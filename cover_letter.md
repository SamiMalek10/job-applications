## Description Of a Claude Skill

Generates compelling, personalized cover letters tailored to specific job postings. Use this skill whenever a user asks for help writing a cover letter, motivation letter, lettre de motivation, or accompanying letter for a job application — even if they just say "help me apply for this job", "write something to send with my CV", or paste a job posting without explicitly naming the deliverable. Also trigger when the user asks to improve or adapt an existing cover letter. This skill analyzes the job description to craft a narrative that aligns the candidate's experience with the employer's needs, in the user's preferred language.


# Cover Letter Tailored

## When to Use This Skill

- Writing a cover letter for a specific job application
- Adapting an existing cover letter to a new role or company
- Writing a motivation letter (lettre de motivation) in French or any language
- Accompanying a CV/resume submission with a personalized letter
- Career change applications requiring a compelling narrative
- Unsolicited applications (candidature spontanée)
- Responding to internship or apprenticeship offers

---

## Instructions

### 1. Gather Information

**From the user, collect:**

- The full job description (or job title + company if no posting available)
- Company name and, if available, the hiring manager's name/title
- The candidate's background: current role, key experiences, main skills, notable achievements
- An existing CV or cover letter if available (use as foundation)
- Preferred language (French, English, or other) and tone (formal vs. warm-professional)
- Any specific angle to highlight (career change, relocation, passion for the industry, etc.)

If the user provides only a job posting and says "write me a cover letter," ask for their background before proceeding — you cannot tailor without it.

---

### 2. Analyze the Job Posting

Extract and prioritize:

- **Key requirements**: Must-have skills, years of experience, qualifications
- **Desired profile**: Soft skills, personality traits, values mentioned
- **Company signals**: Mission, culture, tone of the posting (startup vs. corporate, creative vs. technical)
- **Pain points implied**: What problem is this hire solving? What does success look like in this role?
- **Keywords**: Repeated terms for resonance (not keyword stuffing)

Build a targeting map:

- What does the employer most want to hear?
- What unique thing can this candidate offer?
- What potential gap needs to be acknowledged or reframed positively?

---

### 3. Map Candidate to Role

For each key requirement from the posting:

- Identify the strongest matching experience or achievement
- Find transferable evidence if no direct match
- Choose 2–3 stories or accomplishments to anchor the letter
- Determine the central narrative thread (e.g., "10 years building high-performing teams → exactly what you need to scale")

---

### 4. Structure the Cover Letter

A strong cover letter follows this arc:

**Opening paragraph — Hook & Intent**

- Grab attention: lead with a relevant achievement, insight about the company, or direct statement of fit
- State the position being applied for
- Avoid: "I am writing to apply for…" — too generic
- Aim for: something that makes the reader want to continue

**Body paragraph 1 — Strongest Alignment**

- Showcase the most relevant experience/achievement
- Connect it explicitly to the company's need
- Quantify where possible (managed a team of 12, increased revenue by 30%, etc.)

**Body paragraph 2 — Complementary Value or Differentiator**

- A second angle: a skill, perspective, or experience that others may not bring
- Can address a transition, a passion, or a cultural fit point
- Keep it evidence-based, not just aspirational

**Closing paragraph — Forward Momentum**

- Express genuine interest in the company (mention something specific)
- Invite a conversation / next step
- Thank without being obsequious
- Avoid: "I look forward to hearing from you" (overused) — aim for something warmer and more active

**Sign-off**

- Formal: "Cordialement," / "Yours sincerely,"
- Warm-professional: "Avec enthousiasme," / "Best regards,"

---

### 5. Length and Format Guidelines

- **Standard length**: 3–4 paragraphs, ~250–400 words (1 page max)
- **French format**: Typically includes date, full address block, and object line (Objet: Candidature au poste de…)
- **English format**: Clean block format, no address block required for email applications
- **Tone**: Professional but human — not robotic, not over-the-top enthusiastic
- Avoid: bullet points inside the letter body (prose only)
- Use the candidate's natural voice — ask for tone calibration if unsure

---

### 6. Language-Specific Notes

**French (lettre de motivation):**

- Use "vouvoiement" (vous) by default unless the company clearly uses informal culture
- Standard opening: "Madame, Monsieur," (if name unknown)
- Typical sign-off: "Dans l'attente de votre retour, je reste à votre disposition et vous adresse mes sincères salutations."
- Can be more formal in structure than English equivalents

**English:**

- Warmer, more direct style is increasingly preferred
- First name of hiring manager if known
- Avoid overly formal constructions

---

### 7. Provide a Complete Draft + Brief Commentary

Deliver:

1. The full cover letter, ready to send (or copy-paste)
2. A short note (3–5 bullet points) explaining the choices made:
   - What angle was prioritized and why
   - Any gaps acknowledged or reframed
   - Suggestions for personalization (e.g., "insert a specific company initiative here")

---

### 8. Offer Iteration Options

After presenting the letter, offer:

- Adjust tone (more formal / more personal)
- Shorten to 3 paragraphs
- Rewrite opening with a different hook
- Generate a version for a different role at the same company
- Create a French and English version simultaneously
- Add a specific anecdote or achievement the user wants to include

---

### 9. Best Practices

**Do:**

- Tailor every letter — generic letters are immediately recognizable and dismissed
- Mirror the company's language and values
- Lead with value to the employer, not desire of the candidate
- Be specific: names, numbers, projects
- Keep it honest — never fabricate experience

**Don't:**

- Start with "I" (weak opening)
- Repeat the CV line by line — add narrative, not redundancy
- Use clichés: "passionate," "team player," "hardworking" without evidence
- Exceed one page
- Use the same letter for multiple applications without tailoring

---

### 10. Special Scenarios

**Career Change:**

- Acknowledge the transition directly and reframe as an asset
- Lead with transferable skills and motivation
- Show you've done homework on the new field

**Recent Graduate / No Direct Experience:**

- Lead with academic projects, internships, extracurriculars
- Emphasize learning agility and enthusiasm backed by evidence
- Mention relevant coursework, theses, or competitions

**Unsolicited Application (Candidature Spontanée):**

- Open by demonstrating genuine knowledge of the company
- Explain why this specific company, not just the industry
- Be clear about the type of role sought

**Senior Executive:**

- Shorter, more strategic framing
- Lead with impact and vision, not task lists
- Assume the reader knows what a VP does — focus on *your* approach and results
